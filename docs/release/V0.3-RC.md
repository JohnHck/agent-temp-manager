## B. Package

The candidate package is the CI artifact `release-package`. The job `build package` builds it once from the head of the
pull request, and the six `install e2e` jobs install it, each checking the SHA-256 against the `SHA256SUMS.txt` built
with it. The build refuses an archive unless each of the four gates is a single assignment to `$false`, and it leaves
the CI-only harness `tests/e2e/Invoke-CleanE2E.ps1` out of the archive. The archive is not reproducible byte for byte,
so publication must use that exact artifact, or rebuild and run the whole e2e again.

## F. Not tested (declared limits; maintainer's decision J5)

* **By design or decision (V3):**
  * a write that keeps every statistic (N7);
  * a real exception inside the deleter (O4);
  * a real failure of the log write (L1; no size-limited volume on the runners);
  * an interruption in the middle of the tree (K4; no per-file hook, no forced termination);
  * a base claimed by two accounts (S2; measured: the other account's hive is not loaded).
* **Elevated leg:** its in-deleter cases (`ELIGIBLE` unreachable, because the coverage measured incomplete on every
  runner).
* **Past the gate:** the CLI and the TUI; a human confirmation (every confirmation in CI is automated).
* **Privileges:** not tested in CI: UAC prompts and filtered tokens, credentials of another account and a real
  non-interactive session (SSH, WinRM, scheduled task). The operator's V4 (section G) covered a filtered token, an
  elevated administrator through UAC consent and a cancelled UAC prompt on a real machine. A standard user, credentials
  of another account, a non-interactive session and PowerShell 7 stay NOT TESTED there, with the reasons given in
  section G.
* **Updates:** the e2e covers the published 0.1.0, 0.1.1 and, since PR #74, **0.2.0**. Section G records the 0.2.0
  evidence.
* **Installer race (fixed in the candidate, not in the published installers):** under Windows PowerShell 5.1 the
  environment broadcast after a machine `Path` change compiled its type in the installer's own process, and the
  compiler's temporary files could disappear while the cleanup of the private TEMP walked it. The candidate's installer
  sends the broadcast from a child process. The published installers of 0.1.0, 0.1.1 and 0.2.0, which the e2e runs for
  its cross-version cycles, keep that behaviour: their fresh-install step can still fail intermittently under the
  `powershell` installer shell, with no effect on the product under test. Auditor's decision: the e2e makes ONE new
  attempt of that step only (`xversion-install-<0.1.0|0.1.1|0.2.0>`), only when the failure is "Could not find a part of
  the path" under `<InstallDir>.staging-`, after the uninstaller under test has left the machine clean; the step's detail
  records the attempt and the text. Any other failure, or a second one, stays FAIL; never the installer under test.
* **Proved limits** (SECURITY-MODEL): unobservable handles, a working directory entering a pinned folder, the ACL not
  read again once pinned, and hard links. In each, nothing outside the planned root is affected.
* **Still open:** F-05 and F-14 (deferred since 0.2.0). F-04 is fixed by T1 and counts as closed only once published.
